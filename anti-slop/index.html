about me

Hello, I'm Yashraj Nimbalkar! Before I got into software engineering, I spent years building actual businesses. I learned pretty quickly that a system only matters if it actually holds up in production, under real users and real load.

At 17, I started ClickForAyurveda, an e-commerce website achieving 25–45% in profit margins. I sourced products, built the brand, and handled all the digital marketing, including Facebook ads, Instagram content, and health blogs. But I spent most of my time analyzing unit economics: CAC vs LTV, retention rates, demand patterns. Eventually, competition ramped up, ad costs exploded, and the math stopped making sense. I made the call to shut it down. Not easy, but it was the right move.

I founded Dhani Agro Food Industries, running a tofu production plant for 3 years. Managed the full production cycle and supplied 12 hotels every day. I figured out how to cut production costs from ₹160 to ₹100 per unit through observation, innovation and experimentation. I also got into product innovation & made kalakand (a tofu-based sweet) that originally cost ₹220/kg to make and sold for ₹350/kg. I reformulated it with gulkand and essences, which brought production cost to ₹240/kg but let us sell it at ₹400/kg. That pushed margins from 59% to 67%, an 8 percentage point jump. The daily work involved inventory planning, demand forecasting, supplier negotiations, and making sure quality stayed consistent.

That instinct for taking a system apart and rebuilding it properly is what pulled me into backend engineering. Today I build APIs with FastAPI, handle authentication with JWT and OAuth 2.0, and work with SQL/SQLAlchemy to design the systems underneath a product rather than the storefront on top of it.

I finished my BCom in Banking and Finance with an 8.83 CGPA from SPPU University. Now I'm pursuing an MCA in AI & Machine Learning at Amity University with an ongoing 8.32 CGPA. On the AI side, I study the fundamentals of PyTorch, NLP, and LLMs, and train small models from scratch to understand what happens under the hood: embeddings, attention, and the full training loop. I've also applied this in practice, implementing RAG pipelines and LLM integrations in a few of my own projects, so the AI work isn't limited to theory.

Meridian — HR Assistant Platform

A Role-Aware HR Backend with a Grounded LLM Agent

A full employee-portal backend built with FastAPI and MySQL — JWT auth with role-based access control across six roles, leave and salary management, and an LLM-powered HR assistant that answers employee questions by calling real tools against the database instead of guessing.

Key Details:

  • Agent is hard-constrained to never state a salary, leave count, or date unless it came from a tool call in that conversation — rejects and retries its own output if it detects a fabricated figure.
  • RAG pipeline over company policy documents using sentence-transformers embeddings and a FAISS index, so policy questions are grounded in real documents, not the model's general knowledge.
  • Six-role access control (super admin, HR admin, payroll manager, department manager, HOD, employee) enforced at the route level, with employees scoped to only their own records.
  • JWT access + refresh token flow with bcrypt password hashing and full login history tracking.

Jarvis — Real Time Voice Assistant

A Local Voice Agent with a Live Audio Reactive HUD

A desktop voice assistant that listens, reasons through an LLM tool calling loop, and speaks back using fully offline text to speech, while a browser based HUD animates a 3D blob in sync with the assistant's actual voice output in real time.

Key Details:

  • Audio is streamed to speakers in small chunks rather than played in one call, measuring chunk loudness and pushing via WebSockets to the 3D HUD in real time.
  • Tool calling agent loop resolves multi-step requests and is constrained in system prompts to refuse destructive actions.
  • Offline text-to-speech via Piper, requiring zero external API network requests and zero per-call costs.
  • Plain-text persistent memory file loaded at startup without code changes.
Python LLM Tool Calling WebSockets Offline TTS

Aligned to Whom?

Rejecting Moral Authority in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

A philosophy paper arguing that the dominant AI alignment paradigm rests on a flawed assumption: that human values are stable, coherent, and extractable into code. Drawing on cross-cultural psychology, neuroscience, phenomenology, and political philosophy, it argues that encoding a single moral framework into AI risks freezing one generation's contested worldview into permanent, globally-scaled infrastructure.

Key Ideas:

  • Shows human morality is culturally contingent and only partially accessible even to those who hold it.
  • Proposes "Sovereign Proceduralism," distinguishing defensive alignment (preventing clear harms like violence, fraud, coercion) from ideological alignment.
  • Argues AI should enforce the former and actively avoid the latter, staying transparent about uncertainty and hospitable to disagreement.
  • Concludes AI's role is to remain an honest participant in human moral conversation, not to serve as its arbiter.
